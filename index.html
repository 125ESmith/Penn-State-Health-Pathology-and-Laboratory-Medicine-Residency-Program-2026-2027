Program Highlights

Residents train alongside faculty board-certified across the full spectrum of pathology subspecialties, many of whom hold education leadership roles at the institutional level.

General
  • Faculty-resident mentoring program since 1998
  • Mentored quality improvement projects
  • Faculty and resident wellness committee
  • Resident research curriculum with basic and translational opportunities, support from the Pathology Core Research Laboratory, and departmental and institutional funding
  • Two-year longitudinal core curriculum of AP and CP didactics, including laboratory management
  • Subscriptions to Medical Training Solutions, MediaLab CE, RISE Plus (with question bank) and BoardVitals question banks
  • Support for departmental, institutional and national committee membership
  • Active resident participation in program decision-making
  • Opportunity for an outside elective rotation
Anatomic Pathology
  • Graduated autonomy, with semi-independent sign-out as a senior resident
  • Supervised presentations at interdisciplinary conferences and tumor boards
  • Serve as primary pathologist for one interdisciplinary conference as a senior resident
  • High-volume experience across all specialties: about 40,000 surgicals, 20,000 cytology cases (10,000 Paps, 5,000 fluids, 5,000 FNAs) and 90 autopsies each year
Clinical Pathology
  • Hands-on CP training focused on methodology and clinical consultation to medical services
  • Direct patient responsibilities on the transfusion medicine service
  • Opportunity to earn a Certificate of Apheresis Competence
  • CP attendings rotate service coverage almost daily, giving broad exposure to faculty
  • CP consult rotations with real-time problem solving across laboratory medicine and its management
  • Participation in internal and external CAP laboratory inspections
  • Interaction with postdoctoral fellows in Medical Microbiology and Clinical Chemistry
Fellowships at Hershey: ACGME-accredited fellowships in Dermatopathology, Hematopathology, Transfusion Medicine and Surgical Pathology. Our graduates place successfully in the fellowships of their choice, both at Hershey and at other leading institutions.

Curriculum & Rotation Schedule

The AP/CP program runs in thirteen 4-week blocks per year. It includes 28 structured AP rotations (14 true surgical pathology blocks with grossing, 2 SP selectives and 11 other AP) and 19 structured CP rotations. Residents may begin using electives as early as the PGY-2 year.

Molecular diagnostics is covered within every rotation whose section performs molecular-based assays. Electron microscopy can be done as part of the renal elective.

Residents as Educators

Residents teach as well as learn. They give lectures and unknown conferences, attend Visiting Professor and Distinguished Educator lectures, and help teach medical students in pathology electives and laboratory and PBL/TBL sessions. Additional Resident as Educator sessions are sponsored by the Woodward Center for Excellence in Health Sciences Education together with the Office of Graduate Medical Education.

About Penn State Health

Penn State Health is a multi-hospital health system serving patients and communities across 15 counties of central Pennsylvania. Its mission is to improve health through patient care, research, education and community outreach. Penn State College of Medicine, on the same Hershey campus, partners with the health system on research and education.

611Beds at Hershey Medical Center, the system's flagship teaching and research hospital
722Medical and surgical residents and fellows enrolled
$159MResearch awards funded annually at the College of Medicine
4×Magnet designation for Hershey Medical Center

Distinctions

  • The only hospital in Pennsylvania with a Level I trauma center for both adults and children
  • The region's only heart transplant program
  • The region's only children's hospital, with the highest-level (Level IV) neonatal intensive care unit
  • The first health facility in central Pennsylvania to offer CAR-T cell therapy for adults
  • College of Medicine research on human papillomavirus led to the world's first HPV vaccine
  • Recognized as a national leader in accelerating change in medical education

The Hershey campus

Hershey Medical Center's campus includes institutes focused on cancer, heart and vascular disease and the neurosciences, along with Penn State Health Children's Hospital and the Penn State Cancer Institute. The wider system includes St. Joseph, Holy Spirit, Hampden and Lancaster medical centers and Pennsylvania Psychiatric Institute.

Our legacy

  • 1963

    The M.S. Hershey Foundation offers $50 million to Penn State to establish a medical school and teaching hospital in Hershey. With $21.3 million more from the U.S. Public Health Service, it builds a medical school, hospital and research center in honor of Milton S. Hershey.

  • 1965

    Founding Dean Dr. George Harrell oversees early construction and creates the first Departments of Humanities and of Family and Community Medicine at any U.S. medical school.

  • 1967

    The first class of medical students arrives on September 25, while much of the campus is still under construction.

  • 1970

    The Medical Center admits its first patient on October 14.

  • 1976

    Hershey develops the world's first mechanical blood pump approved to support patients awaiting heart transplant. The Penn State Heart follows in 1985.

  • 1977

    THON, Penn State's student dance marathon, begins supporting the Four Diamonds Fund for children with cancer at the Children's Hospital.

  • 1986

    Life Lion Critical Care Transport launches, the first program of its kind on the East Coast.

  • 1994

    Dr. Eliot Vesell's twin studies of drug metabolism help lay the foundation for personalized medicine.

  • 2010

    An expanded Clinical Simulation Center opens, building on nearly two decades of simulation education.

  • 2014

    Penn State Health, a multi-hospital health system serving central Pennsylvania, is created.

Life in Hershey

Hershey, “The Sweetest Place on Earth,” is a resort town of about 20,000 with a small-town feel, in a growing metropolitan area of roughly 400,000. Harrisburg, the state capital, is 12 miles away. Baltimore, Philadelphia, Washington, D.C., New York City and Pittsburgh are all within a few hours' drive.
